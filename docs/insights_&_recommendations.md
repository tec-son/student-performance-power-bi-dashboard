# Insights & Recommendations

## Executive Summary

Student outcomes are strong overall (98.8% pass rate, 83.5 average), but **101 students (10.1%) are at risk**. Study time and previous grades are the clearest drivers of final scores. Attendance shows only a weak relationship.

## Findings

| # | Finding | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Most students perform well | 98.8% pass rate; 63.8% score A or B (284 + 354 of 1,000) | Maintain current teaching standards |
| 2 | A meaningful group is at risk | 89 D + 12 F = 101 students (10.1%) | Launch an early-intervention programme |
| 3 | Study time is a strong driver | Upward trend line against final score | Promote structured study habits |
| 4 | Previous grades predict outcomes | Positive trend line | Use prior results to flag students early |
| 5 | Attendance is weakly linked to scores | Nearly flat trend line | Do not use attendance alone as a risk signal |
| 6 | Internet access shows a gap | 84 vs 79 average final score (REPLACE: confirm which bar is "Yes") | Improve access for students without it |
| 7 | Parental education has a small effect | Averages range from 83.2 to 85.1 | Treat as weak evidence |

## Recommendations

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| High | Build an at-risk watchlist using previous grades and study time | Both are visible predictors |
| High | Provide study-skills support for low-study-time students | Strongest driver on the dashboard |
| Medium | Expand internet access support | 5-point average gap |
| Low | Review attendance policy impact | Weak relationship with scores |

## Caveats

- Relationships are correlational, not causal.
- The charts show averages; no other variables are controlled for.
- Differences of ~2 points may not be statistically significant. Testing is a suggested next step.
